# Monopoly Official Rules

## Setup

To prepare the game:

- Lay out the board on the playing surface.
- Shuffle the Chance and Community Chest decks and place them face down on their marked spaces.
- Select one player to be the Banker. The Banker manages the Bank's money, title deeds, houses, hotels, taxes, penalties, and auctions. The Banker may also play, but must keep personal assets separate from the Bank.
- Each player selects a token and places it on GO.
- Each player is dealt $1,500: two $500 notes, four $100 notes, one $50 note, one $20 note, two $10 notes, one $5 note and five $1 notes. The exact distribution may differ by edition.
- Each player rolls the dice. The highest total goes first. Ties are rerolled.

## Basic Gameplay

A turn consists of rolling the dice, moving clockwise that many spaces, resolving the action for the space landed on, and then passing play to the next player.

If a player rolls doubles, they resolve the landed space and then immediately take another turn. If they roll doubles three consecutive times, they are sent directly to Jail and their turn ends.

## Types of Spaces

There are two broad types of spaces in Monopoly: properties and action spaces.

### Properties

There are 28 properties in the game, each with a title deed that lists rent, building cost and mortgage value.

- Streets: 22 total, grouped into colour sets. In board order the sets are brown, light blue, pink, orange, red, yellow, green and dark blue.
- Railroads or stations: 4 total, one at the centre of each side of the board.
- Utilities: 2 total, Electric Company and Water Works.

### Action Spaces

There are 12 action spaces:

- 3 Chance spaces
- 3 Community Chest spaces
- 2 tax spaces: Income Tax and Luxury Tax
- 4 corner spaces: GO, Jail, Free Parking and Go to Jail

## Landing on Spaces

When a player lands on a space, the action depends on the type of space.

- Unowned property:
	- The player may buy it by paying the printed price to the Bank and taking the title deed.
	- If the player declines to buy, the Banker auctions the property. Bidding starts at $10 and can increase in increments as small as $1. Any player, including the player who declined, may bid. If no one bids, the property remains unsold.
- Property owned by another player:
	- The owner must ask for rent before the next player rolls. If they do, the landing player pays rent according to the title deed.
	- Street rent depends on monopoly status, buildings and mortgage status.
	- Railroad rent depends on how many railroads the owner has: 1 for $25, 2 for $50, 3 for $100, 4 for $200.
	- Utility rent is based on the dice total that brought the player to the utility: 4 times the roll if the owner has one utility, 10 times the roll if they own both.
- Chance or Community Chest:
	- Draw the top card, follow its instructions, then return it to the bottom of the deck.
	- Get Out of Jail Free cards are kept until used, traded, or sold to another player.
- Tax space:
	- Pay the printed tax to the Bank.
	- Earlier editions allowed Income Tax to be paid as either $200 or 10% of total net worth. The 10% option was removed in the 2008 redesign.
- Free Parking, Jail, or your own property:
	- Nothing happens.
	- Free Parking is only a free resting place and does not award money, property, or any other reward.
	- If a player lands on Jail during normal movement, they are only visiting and may move normally on their next turn.
- Landing on or passing GO:
	- Collect $200 from the Bank.
	- A player may collect $200 twice in one turn if they pass GO and then draw an Advance to GO card.
- Go to Jail:
	- Move directly to the In Jail area. Do not pass GO and do not collect $200.

## Jail

A player is sent to Jail if:

- They land on Go to Jail.
- They draw a card that says Go to Jail.
- They roll doubles three times in a row.

While in Jail, a player does not move, but may still buy and sell buildings, join auctions, collect rent, and trade.

To get out of Jail, a player may:

- Pay $50, then roll and move normally.
- Use a Get Out of Jail Free card, return it to the bottom of the deck, then roll and move normally.
- Buy a Get Out of Jail Free card from another player and use it.
- Attempt to roll doubles. If successful, they leave Jail and use that roll to move, but do not roll again.

If the player fails to roll doubles by their third turn in Jail, they must pay $50 after the third failed roll and then use that roll to move.

## Buying Buildings

If a player owns all streets in a colour set, they have a monopoly. Unimproved rent on those streets doubles, and the player may buy buildings as long as none of the streets in the set are mortgaged.

Buildings may only be built on streets, not on railroads or utilities.

There are two building types:

- Houses, built first
- Hotels, built after four houses

Players may build at any time, not only on their turn. Typical building costs by colour group are:

- $50 for browns and light blues
- $100 for pinks and oranges
- $150 for reds and yellows
- $200 for greens and dark blues

Building rules:

- Players must build evenly across a colour set.
- A street can hold up to four houses.
- Once every street in the set has four houses, a player may convert them into a hotel.
- A hotel is the maximum development level.

The Bank has 32 houses and 12 hotels. If a required building type runs out, further purchases of that type cannot happen until some are returned. If multiple players want the final available buildings, they are auctioned.

## Trading

Players may propose trades at any point in the game, even when it is not their turn.

Trades may include:

- Money
- Properties
- Get Out of Jail Free cards

If a street in a trade is part of a developed colour set, the buildings in that set must be sold first.

The offered player may accept, reject, or counter-offer. Trades may not be used to loan or gift money.

## Help! I Can't Pay!

If a player owes money and cannot pay with cash on hand, they must raise money by selling buildings or mortgaging property.

### Selling Buildings

- Buildings are sold back to the Bank for half of their purchase price.
- Selling a hotel replaces it with four houses.
- Buildings must also be sold evenly across the colour set.

### Mortgaging Properties

- A property may be mortgaged only after all buildings in its colour set have been sold.
- To mortgage a property, turn the deed to its mortgaged side and collect the mortgage value from the Bank.
- Mortgaged properties do not collect rent.
- Mortgaged streets in a monopoly do not stop the other unimproved, unmortgaged streets in that set from charging double rent.
- Railroads and utilities still use their multi-property rent rules even if some of the owner's properties of that type are mortgaged.
- To unmortgage, pay the mortgage value plus 10% interest.
- If a player receives mortgaged property through trade or bankruptcy, they must immediately decide whether to unmortgage it or pay the 10% interest and keep it mortgaged.

## Bankruptcy and the End of the Game

If a player has sold all buildings and mortgaged all properties and still cannot pay a debt, they are bankrupt and leave the game.

- If another player bankrupted them, all remaining assets go to that player, including money, properties and Get Out of Jail Free cards. Mortgaged properties require the new owner to decide how to handle the mortgage immediately.
- If the Bank bankrupted them, the Bank takes all assets. Mortgages are cancelled, cash returns to the Bank, Get Out of Jail Free cards return to the bottom of their decks, and the Bank auctions the seized properties.

The game ends when only one player remains solvent. That player wins.

## Short and Timed Games

### Short Game

The official short game uses these changes:

- At setup, the Banker shuffles the title deeds and deals two deeds to each player for free.
- Only three houses are needed before a hotel can be bought.
- If the edition includes the old Income Tax choice, use a flat $200 tax instead.
- A player sent to Jail must leave on their next turn by one of the normal methods. If they attempt doubles and fail, they must pay $50.
- The game ends when one player goes bankrupt. The remaining players then compare net worth.

Net worth for the short game includes:

- Cash on hand
- Unmortgaged properties at printed price
- Mortgaged properties at half printed price
- Houses at purchase price
- Hotels at purchase price plus the value of the houses surrendered for them

The richest player wins.

### Timed Game

Players may also agree on a fixed play time. When the time limit ends, the richest player wins.

The main setup difference is that the Banker shuffles title deeds, deals two to each player, and players must immediately pay the Bank for those properties.

## Other Notes

- The Bank cannot go bankrupt. If the Bank runs out of money, the Banker may create more using ordinary paper.
